Cancer Therapy Sequelae: Mechanistic & Clinical Research

Researching chronic cancer therapy sequelae? This call supports mechanistic and clinical studies on long-term effects.

The National Cancer Institute is looking for projects that characterize therapy-induced adverse effects.

The scope includes identifying mechanisms and validating biomarkers for future clinical trials.

Both basic science and clinical settings are encouraged to apply.

Are you investigating long-term toxicities or do you know a team that is?

Programme: National Cancer Institute (NCI)
Eligible countries: Any country (non-US organisations eligible) (official call: “Non-domestic (non-U.S.) Entities (Foreign Organizations) are eligible to apply.”)
Who can lead: Any individual(s) with the skills, knowledge, and resources necessary to carry out the proposed research.
Next deadline: 5 Feb 2027 (new applications); later deadlines: 5 Jun 2027, 5 Oct 2027
Official call: https://www.grants.gov/search-results-detail/357015
All open calls, grouped by programme: https://clinicalresearchchaos.com/calls#c-GG-PAR-25-145.

Details are compiled automatically from the official call: always check eligibility, amounts and dates before applying.

Key points:
1. What it funds: This call supports basic, translational, and clinical research to identify mechanisms and characterize chronic or delayed adverse sequelae of cancer therapies. It includes validating biomarkers and clinical endpoints.
2. Who it is for: Higher education institutions, nonprofits, and for-profit organizations are eligible. Any individual with the necessary skills can lead projects, especially collaborations between clinical and basic science.
