Why proxy assessors bias disability ratings

When patients cannot self-report because of cognitive or communication problems, researchers turn to proxies or clinicians. Proxies tend to rate patients as more disabled, while clinicians may use a more disabled reference group. The practical rule: keep the same respondent type at every follow-up. How do you handle respondent discrepancies in your studies?

Key points:
1. Who answers the questionnaire? Assessments may be performed by rehabilitation providers, patients, or proxies, and their ratings can differ.
2. Proxies overestimate disability: Proxy assessors tend to rate patients as more disabled than other functional measures suggest, and the gap grows with more impaired patients.
3. The frame of reference trap: Healthcare professionals tend to rate patients higher than patients rate themselves due to a difference in the frame of reference used.

Takeaway: Proxies rate patients as more disabled than other measures do, so use the same respondent type at every follow-up.
