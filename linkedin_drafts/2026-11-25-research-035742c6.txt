Linear or logistic regression? Look at your outcome

Before running any regression, the first question is never about your predictors—it is about your outcome.

Choosing linear regression means your dependent variable is continuous. Choosing logistic regression means it is strictly binary.

Mismatched models lead to uninterpretable coefficients and invalid statistical conclusions.

When reviewing manuscripts, how often do you see authors force a continuous model onto dichotomous clinical outcomes?

Key points:
1. The outcome dictates the model: Regression helps analyze how various factors connect to health results. Deciding between linear and logistic models depends completely on how you measure your dependent variable.
2. Linear models require continuous data: In linear regression, the dependent variable must be continuous, such as intraocular pressure or visual acuity. Independent variables may be continuous, binary, or categorical.
3. Logistic models need binary outcomes: Logistic regression specifically requires a binary dependent variable, meaning the outcome only has two possible categories, such as yes or no.
4. Comparing the effect metrics: Linear regression reports coefficients showing unit changes in the outcome. Logistic regression expresses predictor effects using an odds ratio to compare relative odds of the event.

Takeaway: Linear regression requires a continuous outcome, whereas logistic regression demands a binary dependent variable.
